# Moments: card anchor, multiple posts, visibility composition, and comments

> Elvis workspace working file, 2026-09-02. Item #11 of the phase-1/1.5 review list. Most of the Moments
> surface was already settled by DEC-015 and by the 2026-08-31 merge (DEC-038 event cover caps, DEC-039
> retention), so this pass covers what those left open, one consequence of DEC-045 that nobody had traced
> into the Moment card, and three provisions sitting in the handoff spec that were never filed as decisions.
> Comparable-app practice researched rather than assumed; sources at the bottom.
>
> No em-dashes. Governance values ALLOW / BLOCK / ESCALATE.

## Already settled, not revisited here

- **Retention**: DEC-039. Tiered, six-month boundary, active at launch, nothing ever deleted.
- **Event cover media**: DEC-038. Five items, separate surface from Moment media.
- **Core content and visibility model**: DEC-015. A Moment inherits its event's visibility by default, the
  owner can override downward, most-restrictive-wins.
- **Eligibility**: DEC-045. Anyone who joined a completed event may post, with no check-in gate.

## The DEC-045 consequence nobody traced, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

Handoff spec §3.5 states that the event anchor frame is **structurally part of the Moment card**, and lists
its contents as four elements: name, date, org, attendance badge.

DEC-045 withdrew the attendance badge. So the anchor component is now **three elements, not four with a
gap**, and it needs redesigning rather than having a slot quietly left empty.

**The denormalization is unaffected**, which is worth stating so nobody "fixes" it: §3.5 copies
`event_name`, `event_date` and `org_name` onto the Moment at creation so the card survives event deletion.
The badge was derived at render time, never a copied field, so removing it changes nothing about what is
stored or about the tombstone behaviour.

## Multiple Moments per event, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

DEC-015 says "one post per user per event". That changes: **a user may post multiple Moments to a completed
event.**

The motivating case is structural rather than volumetric. Someone at a long event may want the afternoon and
the evening as separate posts rather than packing both into one, and a single post forces a false choice
about which half to keep.

**There is no count limit on Moments, confirmed 2026-09-02.** A user may create as many as they want for an
event; what is bounded is their **total media across all of them**, per the cap below. So the constraint is
on volume of media rather than on number of posts, and someone who wants ten Moments of one photo each may
have them.

### The media cap does not change, and this is a clarification rather than a decision

Allowing multiple Moments looks like it multiplies the media allowance. It does not, because **DEC-018's caps
were never per Moment.** `freemium-model-2026-08-19.md` states them as "50 media items **per attendee, per
event**", and gives the reasoning:

> Per-user rather than a shared total, so it genuinely solves the fairness problem a shared pool only
> partially addresses, every attendee independently gets up to 50 items regardless of how many others
> already posted, no "whoever posts after the cap fills gets blocked" dynamic at all.

The cap only *looked* per-Moment because one Moment per event made the two numbers the same object. With
multiple Moments the existing per-attendee-per-event cap simply spans several posts. **Switching to
per-Moment would be a departure from what is decided**, not a continuation of it, and it would remove any
per-event bound entirely. The cost model in the same file assumes the same shape, modelling 8 to 30 items
per attendee per event against the ceiling, so DEC-039's retention economics rest on it too.

### Comparable practice, and why WePop's choice is the better one here

Apple Shared Albums is the closest structural analogue, being many contributors adding media to one shared
occasion. Their limits: **5,000 photos and videos per album, combined across all contributors**, with
per-contributor limits of 1,000 per hour and 10,000 per day *across all albums*.

So Apple caps the collection as a **shared pool**, and its per-person limits are anti-abuse rate limits
rather than fairness allocation. That is the opposite choice from WePop's, and WePop's is better suited
here: a shared pool lets an enthusiastic early poster consume the album's budget before other attendees get
home, which is exactly the blocked-after-the-cap-fills dynamic the freemium doc rejected. Apple can live
with it at 5,000 because nobody realistically reaches it; at 10 or 20 or 50 it would bite constantly.

Instagram is a weaker reference: carousels cap per post with no per-account ceiling, because a personal feed
has no shared occasion to allocate fairly.

## Video length: DEC-015's text is stale, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

Video is **15 seconds for free accounts and 30 seconds for paid accounts, on both Moments and event cover
media**, at 720p H.264. One rule across both surfaces, so a user never has to remember which surface they
are on.

This is already what DEC-038 asserts as standing ("video 15s free and 30s paid"). The problem is that
**DEC-015's own text still reads "a flat 15-second cap and a flat 10-media-item cap for everyone"**, which
was written when the paid tier was deferred and has been overtaken twice since, by DEC-018's tiering and
then DEC-038's restatement. DEC-015 needs its text corrected rather than a new rule invented.

## Visibility: two independent gates that compose

### Org-scoped Moments follow the event's scope, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

Handoff spec §8.2 treated organization-scoped events as a **third case needing its own policy**, and
deferred it as open item O-4. It is not a third case. It is an instance of the general rule.

**A Moment caps at its source event's audience, whatever that audience happens to be.** A public event lets
the author publish anywhere they like. A private event caps at that event's attendees. An org event
restricted to members caps at that event's attendees, who are the members. One rule, three instances, and
**O-4 closes rather than deferring.**

The handoff's schema requirement stands regardless and should still ship: the Moment row carries the source
event's scope from day one.

### Private profiles compose with item visibility, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

Two independent gates, both of which must pass:

| Gate | What it governs |
|---|---|
| **Profile-level** | A private profile shows only name, username, cover photo and background photo to non-mutuals. Mutual followers see the full profile, including Moments. |
| **Item-level** | The Moment's own visibility, capped at its source event's audience per the rule above. |

**Neither gate can be dropped in favour of the other, and the reason is that a Moment is reachable from two
places.** It appears on the author's profile, and it appears on the event page. So someone who cannot see a
private profile may still legitimately reach that person's Moment through an event they both attended.
Profile privacy alone would wrongly hide it; item visibility alone would wrongly expose the profile.
Most-restrictive-wins across both, extending the principle DEC-015 already set.

**Meetup resolves this the same way**, which is worth recording as precedent rather than as coincidence.
Their split: group title, event basics, organiser, cover photo, member count, topics and reviews stay
visible in both public and private groups; member usernames and profile information, event descriptions,
locations, photo albums and discussions are hidden in private groups. And critically, a member can
independently hide their group membership from their own profile, while in a public group member details
stay visible to outsiders regardless of that setting. The item's context governs the item, the profile
setting governs the profile, and neither overrides the other.

**One thing worth taking deliberately from Meetup**: private groups stay **discoverable** in search and
recommendations, because privacy shields the membership data rather than the group's existence. The
analogue is that a private WePop profile should remain findable by name and username, which matches what
stays visible above, so privacy does not quietly become invisibility.

**Instagram's model is the same shape**, though only partly verifiable: their help pages are script-rendered
and the article bodies would not load. What Instagram does state in its own words is that a private
profile's mention on a photo or video is partly visible to a non-follower, which confirms the principle that
**an action on public content stays visible while the profile behind it stays gated.** The rest of the
commonly described behaviour is likely but unconfirmed and should not be cited as fact.

## Comments on Moments, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

**Two orthogonal controls, and separating them removes a whole class of special case.** An earlier draft of
this file tangled them together; Elvis's model is simpler and is what ships.

| Control | What it governs |
|---|---|
| **Moment visibility** (only me / attendees / public) | Who can *see* the Moment, and therefore who is able to comment at all |
| **Comments toggle** (on / off) | Whether comments are *displayed*. When off, only the author sees them, and no new ones can be added |

### Visibility needs no special handling

An earlier draft proposed suppressing the comment affordance on an only-me Moment, on the reasoning that
there is no audience to comment. That is unnecessary. **An only-me Moment has exactly one viewer, so that
viewer is the only possible commenter**, and the box can simply behave normally; the author commenting on
their own private Moment is harmless and is effectively a note to self. Leaving it alone removes a
conditional from both the design and the code, and one state from the model.

### Making a Moment private does not hide its comments

Also from the earlier draft and also withdrawn: there is no hide-and-restore rule tied to visibility
changes. If a public Moment with eight comments is set to only-me, **the comments remain and the author
continues to see them.** Nobody else can reach the Moment at all, so the comments' own visibility is moot.
Hiding is the toggle's job and only the toggle's job.

### The toggle

- **Default on** for public and attendees-only Moments.
- **Off** hides existing comments from everyone except the author, and prevents new ones.
- **On** restores them and allows new ones.
- Copy at the point of turning it back on: **"Turning on comments will restore 8 comments."** Stated once,
  at the moment it becomes relevant, rather than as a persistent badge nagging the author about something
  they deliberately put away.

### Toggle state is stored, not derived

The author's preference lives on the Moment as its own field, independent of visibility. This mattered in
the earlier model and matters more in this one, since the two controls are now fully orthogonal: deriving
one from the other would silently discard a choice the author already made.

### Comments inherit the Moment's visibility

Unchanged from the handoff and still right: a commenter can never be seen by someone who cannot see the
Moment.

## Recap grid: every Moment is its own tile, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

No grouping by author. Each Moment is a separate tile in the event's recap grid.

**The tradeoff is accepted rather than overlooked**, and is recorded so it is not later filed as a bug:
someone who posts eight Moments occupies roughly eight times the grid space of someone who posts one. At a
twenty-person event a single prolific poster can take a visible share of the recap page. Grouping by author
was considered and rejected in favour of the simpler flat treatment.

## Org analytics never include Moment content, RESOLVED 2026-09-02

An org event restricted to its attendees keeps its Moments capped to those attendees. **The organization sees
basic analytics relating to the event, and never the Moments themselves.**

**The org admin receives no elevation.** They see exactly what any user in their position would see, plus the
counts the org is entitled to because the event carries its flag. So an admin who did not join a members-only
event sees general information and counts (how many Moments, how many media items, how much engagement) and
no content: no images, no captions, no author names. An admin who *did* join sees the Moments the ordinary
way, through the event page, as any attendee would.

**Why counts and not content.** Granting content access through analytics would quietly make "capped to that
event's attendees" untrue whenever an org hosts, because an admin who was not there would reach it through a
side door. Counts give the org the operational figure it is paying for without touching the cap, and the
split matches DEC-018's own line between operational numbers and content.

A stronger version, full admin access to org-flagged events, was considered and withdrawn. Honouring it would
have required disclosing at join time and in the composer that club officers who did not attend can see
attendees' photos. **The attendee cap holds with no exception and nothing to disclose.** The admin's
moderation tool is detaching the event from the org, specified in
`workspaces/elvis/org-membership-2026-09-02.md`.

## In the handoff spec, never filed as decisions

These would otherwise be built from a document rather than from the decision log:

- **One media model, three views** (§6.1). The Moment composer is the sole media intake path, with exactly
  one uploader component, one EXIF and GPS stripping pipeline, and one moderation queue. The Event Media tab
  and the recap page grid are **filtered views with no upload of their own.**
- **Tagging requires opt-in consent** (§12.6), as a tag request the tagged person accepts.
- **Moments never display a private venue's exact address** (§12.6).
- **Host takedown is a request routed to review, never an instant delete** (§12.6), with copy that sets that
  expectation.
- **A Moment under review is hidden from public surfaces** with a neutral owner-facing status (§12.6).

## Flags for Deepak

- The Moment card's event anchor is three elements now, not four. No change to the denormalized fields
  (`event_name`, `event_date`, `org_name`) or to tombstone behaviour.
- Media caps are enforced **per attendee per event**, summed across that attendee's Moments for the event,
  not per Moment.
- The comment toggle is a stored field on the Moment, not a function of current visibility.
- Comment hiding is a visibility filter, never a delete. Restoring must bring back the original rows.
- Visibility checks compose two gates and must both be evaluated at render time, since the same Moment is
  reachable from a profile and from an event page by different viewers.

## Not decided here

- **Whether an org event means "created under the org account" or "created by any org member".** The
  analytics rule above assumes the former. The latter would put a member's personal events into every org
  dashboard they belong to, so a student in three clubs would have their private birthday drinks appear in
  three org analytics views. Flagged rather than assumed; the rule as written depends on it.
- Whether an org's analytics distinguish Moment counts on member-only events from those on public events, or
  report them together.
